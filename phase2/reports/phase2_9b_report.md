### Earliest evidence, and what it rests on

The earliest trace of an evaluation-split prediction by the amended pair is
the NTFS birth of `phase2/data/phase2e_eval_sigs.parquet` at **13:27:30.05**.
That is 22 min 48 s after Addendum 1a (`3e79a48`, 13:04:42) and 51 min 14 s
after Amendment 1 (`5f452ad`, 12:36:16). The code that makes those predictions
is no older on disk: `phase2e_mech.py` was born at 13:19:17, `phase2e_run.py`
at 13:21:53, and both modules were first imported together at 13:22:07.

The evidence rests on file-system times on one machine, and it has three limits:

1. Deleting and recreating a file resets its birth time. NTFS tunnelling
   restores the old birth time only within about 15 s. So a birth time bounds a
   file's latest creation, not its first.
2. `mechanism_pass` writes the parquet only after a complete pass over every
   evaluation block. A pass that died earlier would leave no file.
3. Nothing on disk records the pass's start time. The 13:22:07 compile is the
   closest proxy.

No trace found places an evaluation-split prediction of the amended pair before
`3e79a48`. The order amendment, then addendum, then evaluation pass holds on
every trace. The intervals the paper gives (`main.tex:768-770`) are measured to
the run commit at 14:25:07, not to the first evaluation pass, which these times
put at about 13:22 to 13:27. As master plan O3 says, analysis code was edited
after evaluation-split predictions existed. The recorded edits are a crash fix
and a float tolerance (`PASSLOG.md:1460-1468`).
