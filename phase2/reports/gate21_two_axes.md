# Phase 2.1 step 3: the two-axis sanity check (hard gate)

Generated by `phase2/gate21_two_axes.py`. Every number below is computed by that script from `phase2/data/s2and_signatures.parquet`; none is typed by hand.

**Scope.** ADS is computed over the whole ingested subset, not a train split. This pass asks whether the axes have usable range at all, which is a corpus property. The design-time, train-only ADS the spec calls for is a Phase 2.2 concern.

**Nothing here is a mechanism.** No accuracy, no threshold, no cutoff, no winner, no selector was computed.

## Step 2: the frozen blocking normalization

Committed in `phase2/s2and_blocking.py`, with a runnable self-check, before any of the numbers below existed. The rule, in full:

> **block key = first initial + " " + last name, after transliterating to ASCII, lowercasing, and deleting every character that is not an ASCII letter.** A signature whose last name has no ASCII letters is dropped; a signature with no first name keys on the bare last name.

The surface form is frozen in the same file: the signature's own name parts joined by single spaces, whitespace collapsed, case and punctuation and diacritics kept. It is byte-identical to the name printed on the paper (see `gate21_data_shape.md`).

S2AND ships its own `author_info.block` with the same stated intent. We do not use it as the key of record, for two measured reasons:

- It is not a function of the name. 406 of 69,077 distinct (dataset, first, middle, last) tuples map to more than one shipped block, affecting 4,247 signatures (0.55%); hyphens were handled two ways in preprocessing, so 'Murray Gell-Mann' appears under both `m gell-mann` and `m gellmann`.
- It keeps internal spaces and hyphens, so `Gell-Mann` and `Gellmann`, or `De Simone` and `DeSimone`, land in different blocks.

Our key agrees with the shipped field on **98.79%** of signatures and yields 26,939 blocks against the shipped field's 27,658. Coarsening a key raises measured ambiguity, so this choice could in principle buy ADS range; every ADS number below is therefore reported under both keys.

## Axis 2, surface stability: SV

SV(block) = expected normalized Levenshtein distance between two signatures drawn at random without replacement from the block, computed on the raw printed name. Signature-weighted, so a block of many identical strings plus one variant scores near 0. Frozen in `phase2/s2and_blocking.py` before any of this was measured.

Blocks with 2+ signatures (18,984): at exactly 0.0 = **34.36%**, min 0.0000, median 0.0752, mean 0.1048, max 1.0000.

The ADS bands are useless here: SV is bounded well below 1 by construction, because every signature in a block shares a surname. For calibration on the [0, 1] scale of a normalized edit distance, `Jane Smith` against `J. Smith` scores 0.30, `Jane Smith` against `Jane A. Smith` scores 0.23, `Jane Smith` against `Jane Smyth` scores 0.10. Block values in the 0.1 to 0.3 range therefore already mean most pairs differ in form. Its own bands:

## Cross-check against S2AND's own block facets

S2AND defines two block-level difficulty ratios (Subramanian et al., JCDL 2021): *homonymity*, "the fraction of records in the block with the same names that are in different clusters", and *synonymity*, "the fraction of records in the same cluster that have different names". Both are recomputed here from their reference implementation (`s2and/eval.py`), on their normalized full name, not ours.

| comparison | n | Pearson r | Spearman rho |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ads vs homonymity | 18,984 | -0.6638 | -0.8180 |
| sv vs synonymity | 18,984 | +0.5775 | +0.7427 |
| ads vs synonymity | 18,984 | +0.2831 | +0.1797 |
| sv vs homonymity | 18,984 | -0.1636 | +0.0554 |
| homonymity vs synonymity | 18,984 | -0.2234 | -0.1575 |

Figures: `reports/figs/gate21_xcheck_ads_homonymity.png`, `reports/figs/gate21_xcheck_sv_synonymity.png`.

One asymmetry is worth stating plainly: S2AND's synonymity is **label-conditioned** (it counts different-name pairs *within the same cluster*), so it cannot be computed at design time. Our SV is label-free. They are not interchangeable, and the correlation between them is a validity check, not an equivalence claim.

The pattern across those five rows is the one a two-axis claim needs: each of our measures tracks its S2AND counterpart strongly and the other one weakly, and S2AND's own two facets are themselves only weakly related (Spearman -0.157).

## Is the 2-D space actually populated?

A correlation near zero is necessary but not sufficient. Splitting both axes at their medians, the four quadrants contain:

| | SV below median (0.0752) | SV at or above median |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| ADS below median (1.0000) | 1,593 | 3,007 |
| ADS at or above median | 7,899 | 6,485 |

The thinnest quadrant still holds 1,593 blocks, so every combination of the two conditions is represented and a design that varies one while holding the other is possible on real data.

## Step 4: verdict and recommendation

**Consistency axis: alive.** 4,600 of 18,984 blocks (24.2%) sit strictly below ADS 1.0, and they are spread across the whole interval rather than piled on a few small-denominator values: 1,385 in [0.0, 0.5), 1,566 in [0.5, 0.7), 983 in [0.7, 0.9), 666 in [0.9, 1.0), and the 5th percentile is 0.3750. This is the thing WDC did not have.

**Surface axis: alive, and compressed by construction.** 65.6% of blocks have non-zero surface variability, with a median of 0.0752 and a 90th percentile of 0.2637. Its usable range is roughly 0 to 0.5 rather than 0 to 1, because a shared surname bounds how far two names in a block can be apart. The histogram is a spike at 0 plus a broad decaying body, which is a distribution, not a flag.

**Separable: yes.** Pearson -0.2988, Spearman -0.2612, and the sign is the expected one (more surface variety goes with slightly lower purity). Around 7% of rank variance is shared, so they are correlated but nowhere near the same quantity; the association is stable, and modest, inside every block-size stratum, so it is not a block-size artifact. The cross-check against S2AND's own facets shows the same discriminant pattern.

**Recommendation: proceed to Phase 2.2.** Both axes have real range on real data and are separable, so the boundary finding is testable here in a way it was not on WDC. Nothing in this pass licenses a claim about accuracy or about which mechanism wins; that is exactly what 2.2 has to measure.

Carry into 2.2, without acting on any of it now:

- ADS must be recomputed train-only there. These numbers are corpus-level.
- inspire is 15,077 of 18,984 live blocks. Any pooled number is mostly an inspire number, so per-dataset reporting is not optional.
- 29.5% of blocks (the singletons) have no second signature and drop out of both axes. They will also have no history to match against, so how they are handled is a design decision for 2.2, not a silent exclusion.
- SV is bounded below 1 by the blocking key itself. Any threshold on it has to be set from its observed distribution, not from the [0, 1] scale.
- The literature flag from step 0 (Kim and Kim 2020) bears directly on H2 and should be read before the 2.2 hypotheses are frozen.
